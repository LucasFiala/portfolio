Claro From two products to one unified platform

Designed two products from scratch for Claro’s internal teams, a WhatsApp campaign platform and an AI chat agent builder, then merged them into a single journey. Sole designer, from discovery to beta release.

Role
Senior Product designer
Team
1 Designer (me), 1 SM, 1 PO, 2 TLs, 2 QAs, 6 Devs
Tools
Figma, Miro, Claude, GPT
Timeline
Mar 2025 – Oct 2026 (concept to beta release)

The Background

Claro was paying a third-party provider to run its WhatsApp campaigns. We proposed bringing this in-house, and that became IAtende, a platform to create and manage campaigns. In parallel, IA Insights was built to create and manage AI chat agents, with their knowledge bases, departments and reports. The first client was Claro itself, with its internal teams, and the plan was to offer the products to other companies later.

Problem

The two products depended on each other. A WhatsApp campaign starts conversations, and when no human is available, an AI agent has to continue them. So anyone sending campaigns needed both products, with two contracts, two interfaces and two onboardings. On our side, it meant two development teams and duplicated operations.

Solution

I designed both products from scratch and, together with the PO, defended the decision to unify them. IA Insights became the foundation, and campaigns became an optional add-on inside it. The result was one platform, one visual identity and one development team.

Discovery

I interviewed Claro’s teams to understand which tools they used and where they struggled, and ran a benchmark of existing platforms. From there I built personas, user journeys, user flows and the information architecture, then moved to wireframes and prototypes. Decisions were made side by side with the PO and validated with the Tech Leads.

FINDING 1: Costs nobody planned

Teams only discovered what a campaign had cost when the invoice arrived.

FINDING 2: Blocks with unknown reasons

Campaigns and numbers were paused or blocked. Meta had a reason, but the product never made it clear.

FINDING 3: Slow support and confusing data

Questions went unanswered, and the information was hard to find and hard to read.

INSIGHT 1: One journey, two products

Sending a campaign and following its conversations were parts of the same task, split across separate products.

INSIGHT 2: Not everyone needs campaigns

Many clients only wanted AI agents and should not pay for campaign features they would never use.

INSIGHT 3: Consolidation beyond the interface

Unifying the products would also simplify how our own team built and operated them.

Personas

I mapped ten personas in three groups:

  • Campaign users: a Marketing Analyst who runs campaigns daily, a Manager who watches results, budget and the risk of blocks, and a Technical Integrator who connects systems through the API.
  • AI agent users: an Admin who runs the agents day to day, an IT Manager focused on security and compliance, and a Business Manager who checks return on investment.
  • Internal team: Platform Admin, Operations Manager, Back Office and Support.

The internal team was the same for both products, doing the same work twice. That overlap was one of the clearest signs that the two products should become one.

Customer Journey

Before, a client who wanted campaigns had to:

  • Create and configure an AI agent in IA Insights.
  • Open IAtende separately to create and send the campaign.
  • Go back to IA Insights to read the conversations and interactions it generated.

After the unification, all of this happens in one environment.

Information Architecture

The consolidation required rebuilding the navigation almost entirely. I mapped the existing capabilities, identified dependencies between modules, removed redundant screens and steps, and reorganized everything around IA Insights, without simply moving the complexity of one product into the other.

Key Product Decision

A WhatsApp campaign does not end when the message is sent. People reply, and when no human is available to answer, an AI agent has to continue the conversation. That is why every client sending campaigns also needed an agent, and why the two products depended on each other.

The opposite was not true. Many clients wanted an AI agent to answer their chats and had no interest in sending campaigns. Merging everything into one product could force them to pay for a service they would never use.

The answer was to make campaigns an add-on. AI agents are the core of the platform, and campaigns can be switched on for the clients who need them. Each client pays only for what they use, and those who need both get the whole journey in one place.

UI Design

Each finding became a design decision:

  • Unplanned costs → message consumption by Meta category (utility, conversational, marketing, authentication), since each one has a different price, plus an alert when a template is reclassified and its cost changes.
  • Unexplained blocks → template and number quality always visible, with notifications whenever something is paused or changes status.
  • Confusing data → reports and a delivery funnel showing sent, delivered, read and engaged.

On the AI side, each agent works for a specific department and answers only from its own knowledge base, fed by file uploads or synced with corporate cloud storage.

Design System

Claro’s products each had their own look. While designing IAtende, I started a design system to give all of them one visual language. It has been applied to three products so far, and the unification brought the Claro identity to IA Insights for the first time.

The effect showed up in the sprints. Tracking them in Jira, front-end tasks took on average 25% less time to develop, and QA sent back 44% fewer tasks for rework.

Management Layer

I also designed the internal layer used only by the Claro team: onboarding new clients, financial control per client, and a context selector that shows the product exactly as each client sees it.

Delivery and Support

I followed development closely, clarifying decisions and reviewing the build against the Figma files. Support stopped being a black box: our own team became the support channel, with a direct line to Meta, including working sessions at their São Paulo office.

Business Impact

The beta was released in October 2026. Teams praised the interface as far more intuitive and highlighted that they can now find information that used to be hard to get. The consolidation also simplified how the product is maintained: one team and one codebase instead of two, with part of the back end reused.

Learnings

  • Product architecture must evolve with customer needs. Two products made sense at first, but once their capabilities became connected, their boundaries had to change.
  • Consolidation is a product strategy, not just a design exercise. It meant defending a decision that involved customer experience, implementation effort, brand consistency and maintenance cost.
  • Design systems create value beyond one product. What started with IAtende became the foundation for the whole platform.

Next Steps
