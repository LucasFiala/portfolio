Selo UNICEF, Making social impact data usable

Designed a B2B platform that turns fragmented public sector data into clear workflows for managers, schools, and health professionals. Led UX and UI from discovery to delivery, including a scalable design system built from scratch.

Problem

Public managers needed a centralized platform to track vulnerable municipalities. The core challenge was not just displaying data, but highlighting areas where urgent public action was needed, such as vaccination gaps and child protection.

Solution

I designed the Selo UNICEF platform as an intuitive, role based system that organized complex indicators into practical tasks, alerts, and reports for each profile. The experience aligned data views with real responsibilities, for example program oversight for managers, daily operations for schools, and follow up actions for health professionals. I also built a design system from scratch to keep patterns consistent across modules, reduce delivery risk, and support future growth.

Interviews

I conducted semi-structured interviews with public managers, school staff, and health professionals. The goal was to understand how they currently monitored indicators, validated data, and reported progress. I focused on mapping real decision points, the tools they used daily (like spreadsheets), and moments where data gaps forced manual workarounds. I also tested early concepts of role-based navigation to ensure the proposed mental model matched their expectations.

FINDING 1: Different Profiles, Different Needs

Managers required macro-level dashboards for decision-making, while schools and health professionals needed micro-level task tracking.

FINDING 2: Lack of Visual Feedback

Users felt insecure when data was updated without clear status indicators or confirmation, leading to redundant manual checks.

FINDING 3: Technical Jargon

The data came from complex APIs with technical fields that were not translated into human, actionable language for the end-users.

Surveys

To validate the interview findings at a broader scale, I ran a short survey targeting the same three audiences. I asked participants to rank which indicators and actions mattered most for their roles, and to rate early navigation and dashboard concepts. The results helped prioritize role-based landing pages, clearer data-freshness messaging, and faster access to top actions per profile.

INSIGHT 1: Data Trust

Users needed clear visual feedback on data freshness. Knowing when the data was last updated was crucial to trust the indicators.

INSIGHT 2: Task Prioritization

Managers were overwhelmed by raw numbers. They needed the system to highlight which municipalities or schools required immediate action.

INSIGHT 3: Role-Based Views

Schools and health professionals only wanted to see indicators related to their daily routines, not the entire macro view of the platform.

Personas

I created behavior-based personas to align the team on who we were designing for. Instead of demographic labels, I defined the three main audiences by what drove their decisions and what caused friction in their daily work:

  1. The UNICEF Coordinator: Needs a macro view of data and progress to manage resources, engage municipalities, and report on the program’s impact.
  2. The Municipal Articulator: Needs a fast, intuitive way to track school deliverables and support schools, often dealing with low internet connectivity in rural areas.
  3. The Pedagogical Coordinator: Needs a simple platform to map student vulnerabilities and report daily data without technical barriers, as they have low tech affinity and are overwhelmed with daily school tasks.

These personas became a practical tool to evaluate every screen, ensuring the interface adapted to different levels of digital literacy and real-world constraints.

Customer Journey

The end to end journey started when a municipality or partner program needed to track progress against UNICEF Seal criteria and coordinate actions across education and health. Data flowed in from multiple APIs and partners, then stakeholders reviewed indicators to identify gaps, assign responsibilities, and follow up on interventions. The biggest pain points appeared at handoffs, when one team expected another to act but the platform did not yet clarify ownership or timing. The final journey map highlighted moments where the system needed to shift from reporting to action, especially around alerts, status tracking, and evidence for audits.

User Flow

Information Architecture and User Flow for the platform’s core navigation. The structure was designed to separate high-level indicators (for managers) from specific systemic results (RS1 to RS7) and required actions, ensuring a clear path to data validation.

Based on the Customer Journey pain points, I mapped the User Flow to ensure users wouldn’t get lost in complex data. I established a clear hierarchy: global navigation on the left, macro indicators on the dashboard, and a modular structure for the 7 Systemic Results (RS1 to RS7), allowing municipalities to easily drill down from a high-level overview to specific, actionable tasks.

MVP

Initially, stakeholders aimed for a complete, feature-rich application, and an MVP phase was not planned. However, after presenting the first interface concepts, expectations grew, and the scope expanded significantly. Combined with a tight development timeline, we realized the original roadmap was no longer viable.

 

To ensure we delivered a functional product on time, I led the rollback to define a strict MVP. I collaborated with the team to weigh which features aligned with the strategic goals against the actual development effort. We prioritized the core user journeys and essential features, creating a solid action plan that guaranteed the platform’s launch without compromising the user experience.

UI Design

Once the main usability flows were validated with stakeholders, I translated the concepts into high-fidelity UI. The visual direction needed to balance a modern, clean interface with the institutional trust of the United Nations brand. I designed for a desktop-first experience, as managers and coordinators primarily use the platform for complex data analysis in offices.

I applied the core components of the Design System created for this project, ensuring accessible color contrasts, clear data tables, and intuitive dashboard cards. The final interface reflects a deep understanding of the user’s context: it minimizes cognitive load by using progressive disclosure, allowing users to see high-level indicators first and drill down into granular data only when necessary.

Design System

To ensure visual consistency and accelerate development across the platform’s complex modules, I built a comprehensive Design System from scratch. It was designed to serve not just this platform, but to scale for future UNICEF projects, aligning accessibility standards with the organization’s brand identity.

I created a robust library of responsive components, including typography, color tokens, data tables, form inputs, and interactive states, all documented with clear usage rules. This system allowed engineers to reuse code, reduced design debt, and ensured a seamless, intuitive experience for users across all roles.

Usability Testing

Instead of testing with random users, I conducted validation sessions directly with UNICEF Coordinators and key Stakeholders. They were the domain experts who truly understood the daily routines and deliverables required from the municipalities. During these sessions, we walked through the three main user journeys: the Educator, the Municipal Agent, and the Coordinator. The goal was to identify friction points, verify if the information architecture matched real-world logic, and ensure data submission flows were flawless before development.

Problem 1

The user journey was broken for educators and agents, lacking clear checkpoints for deliverables.

Solution 1

I restructured the information architecture, creating specific tasks and progress trackers so each role knew exactly what to deliver and when.

Problem 2

Data submission flows were confusing, leading to errors and lack of trust during the validation process.

Solution 2

I redesigned the submission interface with clear visual feedback, error states, and success confirmations (using the Design System components), ensuring data reached the coordinators correctly.

Learnings:

  • Discovery is non-negotiable. Aligning user needs with stakeholder objectives through research is the only way to design experiences that are truly simple, intuitive, and valuable.
  • Collaboration and iteration are key. Making mistakes is part of the process; the real value comes from transforming those mistakes into better ideas through teamwork and continuous improvement.
  • Fall in love with the user’s problem, not your first idea. Being open to deconstructing early concepts is essential to reaching the best possible solution.

Next Steps:

  • Implement customizable dashboards, allowing each user role to pin the specific indicators and actions they monitor weekly.
  • Introduce automated data quality monitoring to flag missing updates or anomalies, guiding users on the next best action.
  • Expand the Design System documentation for engineering, adding clear accessibility guidelines and code-ready component usage examples.

Business Impact

The digital platform was the backbone that enabled public managers to monitor and act on critical indicators. By transforming fragmented APIs into clear, role-based workflows, the system allowed municipalities to track data efficiently. This operational efficiency translated directly into real-world impact: the 934 certified municipalities outpaced the national average in crucial areas. The platform empowered managers to move from just collecting data to actively improving child immunization, reducing school dropout rates, and significantly increasing the reporting of child protection cases.
